**Reviewer:** Claude · Thursday, November 19, 2026
**Independent review.** I read Handoff.md, Review-Brief.md, Draft-01.md, Draft-02.md and Decisions.md, plus Grok-Review.md (Draft 01) after forming my own view of Draft 02.
**What I read for the series view:** the full locked texts of *The Index Case* (Kindle v2), *The Quiet Line* (Kindle v10), *The Carrier* (Kindle FINAL), *The Reentry* (Kindle v13) and *Common Ground* (Kindle v3, 2026-11-18), along with *The Reentry*'s Development folder (Locked Shape, Chapter Map, Shaping Suggestions, Open Items). ## 1. Bottom line

1. **The Reentry is easy because it was built not to be a mystery.** The locked Open Items say: *"It is not a diagnostic mystery. It is a systems death."* The answer (the pharmacy closes at five and nobody calls) is shown in the Prologue, stated by LANTERN in Chapter 1, used as chapter titles in the table of contents ("Callbacks," "Closes at Five," "Open Late") and printed in the jacket copy. A reader has it by about 5% of the book. The Shaping Suggestions had set the opposite rule: *"the answer is not given away in Chapter 1."*
2. **It isn't only The Reentry.** All five books run on dramatic irony: the reader sees the cause before Wade does. Attentive readers solve every book in roughly the first quarter (table below). That approach has worked for the series because the books are carried by character, ethics and danger. But if Book Six is meant to be a mystery, it needs a different engine, not a better-hidden version of the same one.
3. **Draft 02's carbon-monoxide shuttle is the right kind of premise but not yet a hard one.** It risks being solved early (CO plus "where were they all?" points to a shared vehicle almost at once). The working title names the answer. And it repeats about a dozen investigation beats from *Common Ground*, in the same town. The good news is that Draft 02 already contains the twist that could fix it: the door placard that moves between vans. Build the book around that.

## 2. The series: how each mystery actually plays

| Book | Reader's question | Answer | Reader at ~70% | Wade gets there | Where the suspense really lives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · *The Index Case* | What is destroying healthy livers? | Klotho Leaf "greens" supplement (Orlenwood), plus Grant | Ch 5 (~15%): Diane, double scoop: *"“It’s my spinach,” she said, and smiled, and drank it."* | Ch 12–14 (~35–39%) | Danger to people we know: Ray, Tessa, Grant's eyes, Osei and the Houston liver |
| 2 · *The Quiet Line* | Why did babies die behind a "reassuring" tracing? | The monitor recorded the mother's pulse, and the alarm that would have caught it was switched off under "Quiet Profile" | Ch 7–8 (~22%); a medical reader gets it in the Prologue | Ch 14 (44%) | Shared guilt (Wade's own "Declined"), June, the code |
| 3 · *The Carrier* | What is killing the young Ballou men? | Brugada (SCN5A), and Wade carries it | Ch 4 (~12%): the coved-ST definition said out loud | Diagnosis Ch 9; his own Ch 17–19 | Will Wade protect himself in time? The kinship twist is telegraphed by ~44%, revealed at 83% |
| 4 · *The Reentry* | Why do Laurel Fork patients keep coming back? | Late and weekend discharges, a pharmacy that closes at five, nobody calling, the observation pathway hiding the returns; then the weekend oxygen gap and caregivers who work nights | Core answer by Ch 6 (~20%) in the cold read, most of it already shown in the Prologue and Ch 1 (~2–5%); the "why doesn't it stop" part by Ch 14 (~45%) | Ch 8–9; Ch 28 | Lydia and the scorecard; Nora; the fever scare |
| 5 · *Common Ground* | What is poisoning Tolliver with lead? | Avery's "Common Ground" bowls (the Glass Day glaze) | Ch 2 (~5%): *"Miss Verlie sends me home with the rest, in one of her blue bowls… Eat out of it every night."* | Ch 16–20 (~51–66%) | Bowl recovery, the recall vote, Shay's pregnancy, Margaret |

**What repeats:**

- **A victim-POV opening that shows the cause.** The tub, the blue bowl and the pharmacy grille are each on the page before Wade arrives.
- **LANTERN surfaces the pattern early**, and Wade is the one who lags.
- **Red herrings are things the characters believe, never things the reader believes.** The water in *Common Ground* gets the most pages, but the reader has already seen the bowl.
- **No villain, an M&M confession, and a fix that comes down to one person and one rule.**

Readers who say "too easy" are reacting to that pattern. By Book Four it is recognizable, and *The Reentry* removes even the medical "what is it?" question, which leaves nothing to guess.

## 3. *The Reentry*: specific analysis

### Where the answer is given away

- **Table of contents:** "Seven: Callbacks," "Eight: Closes at Five," "Thirty: Open Late." The front matter answers the question.
- **Jacket copy:** *"…where the pharmacy closes at five, the same patients keep coming back."*
- **Prologue (~1.6%):** the nurse says, *"The pharmacy’s closed… They close at five… They’ll fill them first thing in the morning. They open at nine."* Tonya replies, *"I work nights."* Both halves of the cause and the human cost are already in place.
- **Ch 1 (~4.4%):** Corliss asks, *"Who’s calling them? After. When they go home. Who calls?"* LANTERN answers: *"At Laurel Fork it’s blank in all forty-one charts."* Corliss: *"I can."* For anyone who read *The Carrier*, Corliss's coffee rule ("Every cup's a call") already tells them the fix.

After Chapter 1 the book asks "will anyone act on this?", not "what is happening?" That is a legitimate question, but readers who expect a Dr. Wade *mystery* will find it easy.

### The best mystery material arrives too late and only confirms

The weekend oxygen gap is genuinely good fair play:

- **Planted in Ch 14 (~44%):** Velma Stroud says, *"My tanks ran out… They deliver Tuesdays… Dwayne called them at five. Office was closed."*
- **Paid off in Ch 28 (~80%):** LANTERN finds that nine Friday or Saturday discharges with new oxygen orders were followed by a next ticket dated Monday or Tuesday, and eight of those patients came back first. Inez reads the corner of the faxed ticket: *"That’s Wiley… he doesn’t work weekends because he’s a deacon."*

That is a hidden second cause, found by a person and a machine together. But it arrives at 80% and supports the pharmacy answer instead of overturning it.

**The lesson for Book Six (and for any revised edition):** the shape that would have made *The Reentry* a mystery was already in it:

1. Make "pharmacy plus callbacks" the obvious answer.
2. Fix it around the midpoint.
3. Have the patients **keep coming back** anyway.
4. Make the weekend oxygen gap the real answer, found by Inez and LANTERN.

To support that, trim the Ch 1 "blank in all forty-one charts" line and retitle Chapters 7, 8 and 30. That is a first-solution-fails structure, and it is the main thing the series is missing.

## 4. Book Six: Draft 01 vs. Draft 02

**Draft 01 (*The Next of Kin*)** is a strong novel premise and a weak mystery premise. Grok's review is right that it turns the series inward, and right that the June withdrawal (*The Quiet Line*, Ch 24) is the echo to use. But its question is "what did she mean?", not "what happened?". If the brief is that Book Six **must be a mystery**, the move to Draft 02 is correct. I would bank Draft 01 for a later book, or fold its best element into Draft 02 (see §5D).

**Draft 02 (*The Courtesy Route*) has the right bones:**

- A cluster whose obvious meeting places don't line up.
- Controls who stayed well.
- A source that moves.
- An incomplete roster.
- A dispatcher who is sympathetic and also hiding something.

It is the first Book Six concept in which the reader can genuinely play along. Its risks, from biggest to smallest:

### Risk 1. It will be solved early unless the design changes

- **CO plus "what did they share?" leads a genre reader to a vehicle fast.** Draft 02 itself says *"Wade establishes early that the cases point to carbon monoxide."* Once the diagnosis is named, the source has very few candidates.
- **Act I shows "a driver making room for an extra passenger before the first collapse."** That is the series' victim-POV giveaway again: the tub, the bowl, the grille.
- **The working title *The Courtesy Route* is the answer,** the same mistake as "Closes at Five." Change it before anything goes on a cover or a contents page.
- **One more prime (checked):** in *The Reentry*, Ch 7, Ernesto tells the story of the bus driver Otis, who *"knew when one of them didn’t get on."* It's a lovely seed for the dispatcher, and it's also a hint to returning readers.

### Risk 2. Overlap with *Common Ground* is heavier than Draft 02 allows

Draft 02 already names this comparison. After reading *Common Ground* in full, I'd say these beats would repeat almost one-for-one:

| Beat | In *Common Ground* | Draft 02 equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Same town | Tolliver | Tolliver |
| People who stayed well crack the case | Inez spots the Askins family (the boycotters): *"The sick ones are the supper people."* | Inez builds the comparison from people who stayed well |
| Incomplete roster | Miss Verlie's binder and the Canton guest book (19 of 61) | Dispatcher's off-book riders |
| Safe batch vs. bad batch | September (store-bought glaze) vs. Glass Day bowls | Good van vs. faulty van |
| An out-of-area case breaks the place theory | Margaret | Teenager from another town |
| Wrong public suspect tied to civic stakes | The water plant and the recall vote | The county "checking buildings" |
| A test that falsely reassures | Kittrell misses the bowl | (CO equivalent: a pulse-ox reading that looks normal) |
| Recovery act | Door-to-door bowl recovery | Tracing riders |
| Well-meaning source | Avery | Dispatcher |

Moving the source is a real difference, but the investigative choreography is the same. **At minimum:** use a new town, and don't let Inez win the case with the stayed-well comparison again. Give that beat to someone else, or make it fail.

### Risk 3. A third "systems, no villain" book in a row

*The Reentry* (rules bent by kind people: Corliss's calls, the deacon who doesn't work weekends) and *Common Ground* (a generous potter) both resolve to "good people and a gap." Draft 02's broader question, *"what happens when people depend on a public service that works partly because someone bends its rules?"*, is almost exactly the *Reentry* thesis. A mystery needs **someone who knows something and isn't saying it**, with a reason that matters. In five books, no witness has ever actively lied to Wade. That is the missing ingredient.

### Continuity checks for Draft 02

- **Josie Tate does not appear in *Common Ground*** (0 mentions, checked).
  - Her last on-page status is *The Reentry* Ch 25: her nurse-anesthesia interview in January, with Dale as her reference.
  - The Reentry Shaping Suggestions flag *"She may not finish"* as a reveal held for later in the series.
  - Draft 02's plan to bring her "from Caddo Pines" needs a check on where she is in time and place, and whether Book Six is where that reveal lands.
- ***Common Ground*'s main action is March 17 to May 18, with an epilogue on October 18.**
  - Book Six therefore falls naturally in late fall or winter: heater season and flu season.
  - That helps: CO poisoning hides inside a winter of headaches, nausea and "the flu that's going around." (Proposal.)
- **LANTERN** (per the *Common Ground* text and continuity file):
  - It is on a one-year Pinewood contract from March, with renewal "next spring." Book Six has to say where that stands.
  - It sees **Laurel Fork charts only**. A route that crosses town lines means some riders were seen at hospitals LANTERN cannot see. That is useful: it builds the incomplete roster into the data as well as the paper.

## 5. How to make Book Six a real mystery (proposals)

**A. Give the reader a rival answer that holds until the midpoint.**
- Keep CO, but don't name it "early." Push the diagnosis to about 20–25%, behind a winter cluster that looks like flu.
- Then give the county's building theory **real evidence**: one building, such as the library, shows a marginal reading or has a flue fault. The public answer appears confirmed and the building is closed.
- The reader should believe it too, not just the characters.

**B. Use the reversal Draft 02 already contains: the placard moves.**
- Draft 02 notes *"The route had the same name and the same door placard on both days."* Make that the hinge of the book.
- The county pulls the van with the placard. Two days later another rider goes down, because the placard (and the route) moved to the borrowed vehicle with the exhaust fault, or because the "fixed" van came back from the shop early.
- That is the first-solution-fails turn the series has never used. Place it at about 50%.

**C. Put a human secret at the center.**
- Someone moved the placard, signed the repair off early, or put a rider on board who shouldn't have been there. They hide it for a reason we sympathize with: losing the route, losing their job, protecting someone.
- Not a villain. A withholder.
- Interviews then become contests rather than information delivery. The dispatcher is the obvious candidate, so consider making the withholder someone the reader trusts: the mechanic, the driver, or one of the people who stayed well.

**D. Fold Draft 01's best element in.**
- Make one of the first patients a woman found down who **cannot say where she was that morning**. Her inability to answer is the mystery, and Draft 01's "we can no longer ask her" grief becomes the engine of the investigation.
- Her daughter, driving in, knows her routine and supplies the "baseline detail" Draft 01 wanted. She could also be the unlisted rider whose fate closes the book.

**E. Fair-play rules for the drafting team.**
- No prologue or POV scene that shows the true source. If there is a cold open, it should show the **false** location.
- No chapter title, jacket line or working title that names the cause.
- Every true clue goes in Wade's line of sight, with a false clue of equal weight beside it. Doctor-fair clues readers can play with (proposals to verify with clinical sources):
  - A normal pulse-ox in a patient who looks hypoxic.
  - Headaches that improve on days with no ride.
  - The rider who always takes the front seat stays well.
  - A dog or small child gets sick first.
- LANTERN should **not** be the one to state the answer. Let it be wrong once, as in *Common Ground*, but about the right thing.

**F. Test it before chapters.** Once there is an outline, run the cold-read test on it: a reader who doesn't know the answer marks, chapter by chapter, when they'd guess it. Target: a sharp reader shouldn't pass 50% confidence before the midpoint reversal.

## 6. Cold-read test of *The Reentry*

I had a separate reader go through *The Reentry* in order, with no access to the development notes, logging a best guess and a confidence after each chapter. The quotes below are checked against v13.

**The book has two questions:**
- **Q1:** Why do the patients keep coming back?
- **Q2:** Why doesn't the loop stop?

**Guess timeline:**

| Point in the book | Reader's best guess | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Royce will die from a missed water pill | 60% for his case |
| Ch 1 | LANTERN all but states the answer: *"A lot of them go home… on a Friday or a Saturday… There’s a hole in every chart where home is."* | 55% |
| Ch 4 | Marisol, watching the grille come down: *"Is that it?"* | 65% |
| **Ch 6 (~20%)** | **Q1 solved.** Clues: the observation pathway, the "Network Strategy and Planning" footer, the woman in good shoes, Inez's *"We do now."* | **~75%** |
| Ch 7 | Night-working caregivers named outright: Wade asks, *"What if the daughter works nights?"* | (seeded) |
| Ch 9 | *"Last modified by: LSAYERS"* confirms Q1 | 90% |
| **Ch 14 (~45%)** | **Q2 solved.** Velma's tanks give away the oxygen piece, delivery day included. | **~70%** |
| Ch 15–27 (46–82%) | No new evidence on the mechanism | 75–80% |
| Ch 28 | Revealed as expected | Confirmation |

**What the reader concluded:**

- **The stall:** Wade knows less than the reader for about a third of the book.
- **Rivals don't last:** no rival explanation stays credible past about 30% of the book.
  - Lydia is cleared and made sympathetic by Ch 9–10.
  - The locum's extra-pill advice and the insurer's "refill too soon" rejection are each raised and dropped within a chapter.
- **"Closes at five" is overused:** it recurs in at least eight chapters.
- **The suspense that works is not the mystery:** Wade's fever (Ch 16–17), Lydia's dilemma, and "who calls when Corliss retires." Ch 21–25 (Nora and Frances) are emotionally strong but add nothing to either question.

**The reader's fixes** (useful for a revised edition, and as a checklist for Book Six):
1. End the Prologue before the empty pill bottle.
2. Cut "Friday or a Saturday" from LANTERN's Ch 1 speech.
3. Delete "Is that it?" (Ch 4) and two or three of the later "closes at five" refrains.
4. Remove "What if the daughter works nights?" (Ch 7) and "works nights" from Ebersole's note, so the night-shift caregiver pattern is a real discovery in Ch 28.
5. Have Velma blame only the power outage, leaving the Tuesday delivery schedule for LANTERN to find.
6. Develop a rival cause for the 40–60% stretch: the departed locums, or systemic "refill too soon" rejections.
7. **Add a reversal:** move "Open Late" earlier as a pilot that barely changes the returns.

The reversal in item 7 is the same first-solution-fails structure I recommend in §3, reached independently.

## 7. Priorities

**Preserve:**
- The moving source and the incomplete roster.
- The dispatcher's own point of view.
- Wade being "hard to work with," seen through Inez's eyes.

**Changes, ranked:**
1. **Build a midpoint reversal (the placard)** so the first solution fails. This is the single biggest fix for "too easy," for this book and for the series.
2. **Add a withholder with a sympathetic reason,** so interviews become contests and the book isn't a third "systems, no villain" story.
3. **Get out of *Common Ground*'s shadow:**
   - A new town.
   - Inez doesn't win with the stayed-well comparison again.
   - A new working title that doesn't name the answer.

**Scene to sketch first:** the morning after the county pulls the van. Wade has told the family it's over. A new patient comes in by ambulance with a cherry-red nothing: normal oxygen saturation, a headache, and a seat on the route that afternoon. Inez looks at the placard on the van parked outside the ER. If that scene lands, the book has a middle.
